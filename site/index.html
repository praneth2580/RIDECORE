Screens

Swipe it like a watch

Every screen fills the phone. Swipe between them: each snaps into place, and dots show where you are. The dash is home, with a screen on each side of it: everything is a swipe away, and the way back is the way you came. Press Home, leave it alone for 30 seconds, or come up to a turn, and it's back on its own.

  1. Pull down

    Settings

    Big keys, lit when on: what the dash shows, voice, turn glow, auto-record, how the phone is mounted.

  2. Home

    Dash

    Speed, the map with the next turn over it, and the trip. Tap the map to look around.

  3. Swipe up

    Apps

    The phone's other apps, as on any home screen. Hold one to pin it to the top.

  4. Swipe right

    Music

    What's playing, with big keys to play, pause and skip. Radio stations, favourites first.

  5. Swipe left

    Where to

    Your last places, one tap each, and search. On a trip: the turns ahead.

  6. 2 swipes left

    Rides

    One big face to record and pause; paused, save or discard. Under it, every ride you've recorded.

Features

Made for a phone on a mount

  • A dash you read at a glance

    Speed in big numbers, the next turn over the map, time to go and arrival beside it. The weather on the top line, with an umbrella when rain is coming. Signal, GPS, your headset and the battery in the corners round the dials, with a word only when something's wrong. Switch off any part you don't want, and the rest takes its room.

  • A map that rides with you

    It follows you, zooming out as you speed up and back in as you slow down. Pick how far: ×0.5, ×1 or ×1.5. Dark, with your route in green.

  • One tap to go again

    Typing on a mount is slow, so your last six places come first, a tap each. Search is there for somewhere new.

  • Routes for bikes

    Fastest, Shortest or Less traffic, with up to three routes to compare. Expressways closed to two-wheelers stay off unless you allow them.

  • Voice turn-by-turn

    Prompts timed to your speed, into your helmet. Miss a turn and it finds a new route. Routes are worked out on the phone, so guidance keeps going when the signal drops.

  • Turns in the corner of your eye

    As a turn comes up, the screen's edge on that side glows, brighter as you get closer, and pulses when it's time. Arriving lights both sides green.

  • A ride recorder

    Tap the big face to record; it shows the distance, time and average speed. It keeps going with the screen off, and keeps each ride with its distance, moving time and top speed. Or let every trip record itself.

  • Music on the ride

    What any music app on the phone is playing, with big keys to play, pause and skip. Or internet radio in RIDECORE itself: star the stations you like.

  • A real home screen

    Make it the phone's home app. Home brings the dash back, Back never leaves it, and your music, calls and other apps are a swipe up.

  • Mounted your way

    Landscape or portrait, chosen the first time you open it and held either way up. It fills the whole screen, with Android's bars hidden, and stays on: always, or only while you navigate.

Set up

Set up a riding phone

  1. On the phone, tap Download and open the file (about 10 MB). If Android asks, let your browser install apps (Install unknown apps), then tap Install.
  2. Open RIDECORE, allow location, and pick how the phone sits on the mount: landscape or portrait.
  3. Pull down from the dash for Settings, tap Home app and choose RIDECORE. Home now brings you to the dash.
  4. Mount it and plug it in. The screen stays on, and the battery shows at the top.

RIDECORE isn't on Google Play, so Android may warn you about installing it. To update, install the newer file over the one you have: your rides and settings stay.

FAQ

Questions riders ask

Is RIDECORE free?

Yes. There's no account to create and no ads.

Which phones does it run on?

Android 6.0 or newer, with GPS. There's no iPhone version. An old phone you no longer carry is ideal: RIDECORE is made to be the only thing on it.

Can I still use my other apps?

Yes. Swipe up from the dash for all of them: tap one to open it, hold one to pin it to the top. Home brings you back to the dash.

How does music work?

Swipe right from the dash. RIDECORE shows what any music app on the phone is playing, with keys to play, pause, go back and skip. To see the song's name, allow it once in Settings → Music info; the keys work without it. Or play internet radio from RIDECORE itself: your starred stations first, then the most played in your country. One plays at a time.

How do I get my old home screen back?

Pull down from the dash for Settings and tap Phone settings. In Android's settings, open Apps → Default apps → Home app and pick the one you had. The names differ a little from phone to phone.

Does it work without signal?

Partly. Routes are worked out on the phone, so once a trip is planned, turn-by-turn and voice prompts keep going without a connection. Searching for a place, planning a trip and loading the map for new areas need one.

Is it finished?

Version 1 is out and does everything on this page. It's young, so please tell us what breaks.

What's TrailCompass?

RIDECORE's engine. TrailCompass is a motorcycle navigation app by the same developer. RIDECORE uses its routing, turn-by-turn, voice prompts, GPS and ride recording, and builds its own device around them.

Where do the map and place data come from?

Maps, roads and search come from OpenStreetMap, through OpenFreeMap, Overpass and Nominatim. Fuel stations and places to eat (Settings → Fuel & food) come from the Overture Maps Foundation and OpenStreetMap. The weather comes from Open-Meteo, and radio stations from Radio Browser.

Does it collect my data?

There's no account and no analytics. Your rides, places and settings stay on the phone. To show the map, plan routes and search, the app fetches data for the areas you use from the open-data services above. The weather is asked for with your position rounded to about 5 km.

Ride safe

RIDECORE is not a certified navigation or safety system. GPS and map data can be wrong or missing. Choose your route before you ride, and don't work the phone while you're moving: it's made to be glanced at.
